**This project is a work in progress. More will be added as it develops.**

I am an undergraduate research assistant in Cornell's ASTRA Lab aerospace laboratory, where I am working on the design of COMET, the Cornell Orbital Material Environmental Testing chamber. The chamber is intended to simulate the temperature and radiation conditions of space so that materials can be tested under them.

## Design goals

The chamber has to reproduce the space conditions we care about closely enough to give meaningful material test results, while staying within the project's budget.

## Process so far

My work so far has focused on turning those goals into a design that can actually be built:

- Evaluating possible chamber configurations against the requirements of the testing we want to do.
- Reviewing the relevant literature to inform the final design selection.
- Researching component vendors and comparing parts and their specifications against the system requirements and budget.
- Supporting chamber assembly and planning the calibration of its sensors and temperature-control systems.

Each of these decisions involves trade-offs, because a choice that helps one part of the system can affect another. The process has meant defining the requirements clearly first, and then weighing the options quantitatively against them.
